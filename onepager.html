For Soo-Jin · GDI Board Meeting · May 13, 2026

What the Salesforce program delivers — for Kristy and for Grant

Thirteen board-grade benefits surfaced through nine weeks of discovery and scoping with LeafGuard, Patio Enclosures, and Champion Windows. Six on the front of the funnel (Kristy / CMO); seven in retail operations (Grant / CRO). Every operator quote is verbatim.

Kristy · CMO

Lead to Appointment

Marketing, lead intake, call center, attribution.

  1. Marketing finally gets a "shopping cart" it can test on. Chris Jarvis (Director of Digital Marketing): "If I think about an e-com business, they're constantly testing on their shopping cart. This is effectively our shopping cart and we have very little ability to test on it."
  2. The current tech stack costs users headache and time — Salesforce becomes one pane of glass. Justin Hennessey (VP Operations, PE): "We're in three different systems so far, trying to set a damn appointment that is the lifeblood of a business."
  3. A unified lead taxonomy delivers cleaner cross-brand reporting — and ends the daily manual cleanup. Melissa Skinner (VP Marketing, PE): "We could make somewhere between 10 and 100 adjustments on any given day." David Rosser (President, LG) on the taxonomy itself: "LeafGuard, LeafGuard Rehash, LeafGuard Costco, LeafGuard Canvassing — you have to know how to work the system to pull a report that's accurate."
  4. Consent management out of Excel — 100% enforcement, regulatory risk drops. Alex Grist (PE Client Experience) on the manual do-not-contact tracker she maintains today: "A major lift that we need help with for sure." Melissa Skinner adds that the current platform forces opt-IN as default — direct TCPA exposure on the way out.
  5. Installations aren't living up to marketing's potential. David Rosser: "Fifty percent of the time, they would be able to see that this isn't LeafGuard. Today we're rolling trucks every time a customer calls." Marketing dollars wasted; brand erosion at the first-line service moment.
  6. A unified customer experience enables cross-selling across brands and inter-brand learning. Pairs directly to Soo-Jin's own frame: "Cross-sell, upsell is the name of the game for Great Day right now." One household, multiple brands, one platform.

Grant · CRO

Sale to Service

In-home sale, install, change orders, service, warranty, AR.

  1. $2.25 B of unworked quoted pipeline at PE becomes visible. Mike Moore (PE): "In 2024 we wrote $2.5 B of quotes and sold $250 M of those. There's 85% just hanging out there."
  2. Real-time job-cost variance closes the margin leak. Mike Moore: "Three or four points worth of margin on a $300 million business." Last month $1 M in expenses landed on jobs already claimed for revenue.
  3. The customer stops being the system's bug-tracker.New Justin Hennessey (PE), asked how service scheduling fits in: "When they're screaming." Mike Moore: "Closing services out is a hit and miss process… nobody's going back into the system to complete it." Today, fractured platforms route work to the wrong queue and there's no system gate that says a job is 100% done — so the customer is the discovery mechanism. Salesforce Service Cloud + Field Service workflow gates close every job to a tracked state and trigger cases automatically when completion criteria aren't met.
  4. Service & warranty stop being a black box — ~$2 M/year of upsell becomes attributable. Mike Moore: "A couple million dollars a year out of our service referrals" — 65 years of installs, single-pane upgradable units, screen-room-only units, all upsell-ready.
  5. Service A/R stops being silently written off. Mike Moore: "Service AR sits out there and ends up being just written off because we already took it and applied it to something else."
  6. Champion's 20-variant warranty interpretation problem gets standardized — a direct margin lever. Kelly Meyers (CHW): "Quite frankly, I'm sure we're not charging the way that we should be when it comes to some of our consumers."
  7. LG installer economics become measurable for the first time. David Rosser & Amanda Opara: 30,000 installs/year at ~$12 K average — sold at 3–4× competitor pricing. $1,200/hour revenue per crew. 98% one-call close. Amanda: "That's a report that we want and we need." None of it captured as a system metric today.
