For Soo-Jin · GDI Board Meeting · May 13, 2026

What the Salesforce program delivers — for Kristy and for Grant

Twelve high-level benefits surfaced through nine weeks of discovery and scoping with LeafGuard, Patio Enclosures, and Champion. Six on the front of the funnel (Kristy / CMO); six in retail operations (Grant / CRO).

Kristy · CMO

Lead to Appointment

Marketing, lead intake, call center, attribution.

  1. Marketing finally gets a "shopping cart" it can test on. Chris Jarvis (Marketing Tech): "If I think about an e-com business, they're constantly testing on their shopping cart. This is effectively our shopping cart and we have very little ability to test on it."
  2. The current tech stack is causing a lot of headache and time-spend for users — Salesforce becomes one pane of glass for the agent. Chris Jarvis: "Hatch SMS, HeavySet emailing, RingCentral channels — agents bouncing between windows all the time."
  3. A new unified lead taxonomy will deliver cleaner reporting on KPIs across brands. David Rosser (LG): "LeafGuard, LeafGuard Rehash, LeafGuard Costco, LeafGuard Canvassing — you have to know how to work the system to pull a report that's accurate."
  4. Consent management out of Excel — 100% enforcement, regulatory risk drops. Alex Grist (PE Client Experience) on the manual do-not-contact tracker she maintains today: "A major lift that we need help with for sure."
  5. Installations aren't living up to marketing's potential. David Rosser: "Fifty percent of the time, they would be able to see that this isn't LeafGuard. Today we're rolling trucks every time a customer calls." Marketing dollars wasted, brand erosion at the first-line service moment.
  6. A unified customer experience will enable cross-selling across brands and learning from inter-brand success. Pairs directly to Soo-Jin's own frame ("Cross-sell, upsell is the name of the game for Great Day right now"). One household, multiple brands, one platform.

Grant · CRO

Sale to Service

In-home sale, install, change orders, service, warranty, AR.

  1. $2.25 B of unworked quoted pipeline at PE becomes visible. Mike Moore (PE): "In 2024 we wrote $2.5B of quotes and sold $250M of those. There's 85% just hanging out there."
  2. Real-time job-cost variance closes the margin leak. Mike Moore: "Three or four points worth of margin on a $300 million business." Last month $1M in expenses hit jobs already claimed for revenue.
  3. Service & warranty stop being a black box — ~$2M/year of upsell becomes attributable. Mike Moore: "A couple million dollars a year out of our service referrals" — driven by 65 years of installs, single-pane upgradable units, screen-room-only units.
  4. Service A/R stops being silently written off. Mike Moore: "Service AR sits out there and ends up being just written off because we already took it and applied it to something else."
  5. Champion's 20-variant warranty interpretation problem gets standardized — a direct margin lever. Kelly Meyers (CHW): "Quite frankly, I'm sure we're not charging the way that we should be when it comes to some of our consumers."
  6. LG installer economics become measurable for the first time. David Rosser & Amanda Opara: 30,000 installs/year at ~$12K average — sold at 3–4× competitor pricing. $1,200/hour revenue per crew. 98% one-call close. None of it captured as a system metric today.
