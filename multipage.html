For Soo-Jin · GDI Board Meeting · May 13, 2026

Why Salesforce. Why Now.

Nine weeks of structured discovery and scoping across LeafGuard, Patio Enclosures, and Champion Windows have produced one consistent answer: GDI's current systems are not failing — they are actively costing money, suppressing growth, and making the enterprise vision impossible to operate. Salesforce is the platform built to carry it.

The case in plain terms. Across three brands and three weeks of structured discovery, the same story surfaced in three different buildings from three different leadership teams. Revenue is leaking. The business is running on instinct, not data. The current systems have hit their ceiling. The enterprise vision has no technical home. Delaying does not preserve the status quo — it extends the cost of it.

The Thirteen Benefits

What the Salesforce program delivers — for Kristy and for Grant

Six on the front of the funnel (Kristy / CMO). Seven in retail operations (Grant / CRO). Every operator quote is verbatim from a discovery or scoping session.

Kristy · CMO

Lead to Appointment

Marketing, lead intake, call center, attribution.

  1. Marketing finally gets a "shopping cart" it can test on. Chris Jarvis: "If I think about an e-com business, they're constantly testing on their shopping cart. This is effectively our shopping cart and we have very little ability to test on it."
  2. The current tech stack costs users headache and time — Salesforce becomes one pane of glass. Justin Hennessey (VP Operations, PE): "We're in three different systems so far, trying to set a damn appointment that is the lifeblood of a business."
  3. A unified lead taxonomy ends the daily manual cleanup and unlocks cross-brand reporting. Melissa Skinner (VP Marketing, PE): "We could make somewhere between 10 and 100 adjustments on any given day." David Rosser: "LeafGuard, LeafGuard Rehash, LeafGuard Costco, LeafGuard Canvassing — you have to know how to work the system to pull a report that's accurate."
  4. Consent management out of Excel — 100% enforcement, regulatory risk drops. Alex Grist (PE Client Experience): "A major lift that we need help with for sure." Melissa Skinner adds the current platform forces opt-IN as default — direct TCPA exposure on the way out.
  5. Installations aren't living up to marketing's potential. David Rosser: "Fifty percent of the time, they would be able to see that this isn't LeafGuard. Today we're rolling trucks every time a customer calls." Marketing dollars wasted; brand erosion at the first-line service moment.
  6. A unified customer experience enables cross-selling across brands and inter-brand learning. Pairs directly to Soo-Jin's own frame: "Cross-sell, upsell is the name of the game for Great Day right now." One household, multiple brands, one platform.

Grant · CRO

Sale to Service

In-home sale, install, change orders, service, warranty, AR.

  1. $2.25 B of unworked quoted pipeline at PE becomes visible. Mike Moore (PE): "In 2024 we wrote $2.5 B of quotes and sold $250 M of those. There's 85% just hanging out there."
  2. Real-time job-cost variance closes the margin leak. Mike Moore: "Three or four points worth of margin on a $300 million business." Last month $1 M in expenses landed on jobs already claimed for revenue.
  3. The customer stops being the system's bug-tracker.New Justin Hennessey, asked how service scheduling fits in: "When they're screaming." Mike Moore: "Closing services out is a hit-and-miss process… nobody's going back into the system to complete it." Today, fractured platforms route work to the wrong queue and there's no system gate that says a job is 100% done — the customer is the discovery mechanism. Service Cloud + Field Service workflow gates close every job to a tracked state and trigger cases automatically when completion criteria aren't met.
  4. Service & warranty stop being a black box — ~$2 M/year of upsell becomes attributable. Mike Moore: "A couple million dollars a year out of our service referrals" — 65 years of installs, single-pane upgradable units, screen-room-only units, all upsell-ready.
  5. Service A/R stops being silently written off. Mike Moore: "Service AR sits out there and ends up being just written off because we already took it and applied it to something else."
  6. Champion's 20-variant warranty interpretation problem gets standardized — a direct margin lever. Kelly Meyers (CHW): "Quite frankly, I'm sure we're not charging the way that we should be when it comes to some of our consumers."
  7. LG installer economics become measurable for the first time. David Rosser & Amanda Opara: 30,000 installs/year at ~$12 K average — sold at 3–4× competitor pricing. $1,200/hour revenue per crew. 98% one-call close. Amanda: "That's a report that we want and we need."

The Window

Why now — not later

The case for moving now

  • Discovery across three brands is complete. Design begins July 23. Go-live February 1, 2027 — hard, non-negotiable. Every week of delay compresses against that date.
  • The current band-aids are already failing. Hatch, HeavySet, Excel-based scheduling, manual marketing attribution corrections — each was acknowledged in discovery as a stopgap. "Hatch was designed as a band-aid. It was just a bridge application to begin with." (Chris Jarvis)
  • The organizational readiness exists today. LeafGuard is pulling for the platform; Patio Enclosures leadership participated in three full days of structured sessions; Champion is current and engaged. Rebuilding this momentum after a pause is the costlier path.
  • The delivery approach itself is the risk mitigation. Discovery across 6 brands → Global Operating Model designed once → focused implementation on LG (likely with PE). The hybrid avoids both the eleven-brand-waterfall risk and the brand-by-brand-agile-into-incompatible-builds risk. Twelve cross-brand foundation locks already confirmed by three operators.
  • Governance is settled — in operator language. Brian Perry: "Post-sale RevOps decisioner is Grant. Pre that is Kristy. Soo-Jin resolves impasse. Brian is point of contact before C-suite." Soo-Jin: "For marketing and call center it'll be Kristy. For retail operation it'll be Grant. I'll be the referee, and Jordan will bring it up to the tech steering committee."
  • The KPI scoreboard is locked. Five Charter Success Metrics, GDI-authored, leadership-validated: Set Rate, Speed-to-Lead, Consent Enforcement, Brand Visibility, Executive Reporting. Lead Conversion Rate explicitly removed.

How we're set up to deliver

  • Salesforce is the platform, not the only answer. Modular by design — build inside Salesforce where it's best-of-breed; integrate where the operators have working tools. Current direction (still firming up): Folio V2 for in-home sales at PE; Salesforce Field Service mobile + a dedicated contractor portal for install/service; Workato as the integration abstraction layer.
  • Three brands, one answer — twelve foundation locks. Confirmed independently by LG, PE, Champion. When three operating models converge, it's a foundation lock, not a brand quirk.
  • Champion is the in-house proof, not a complication. Already runs the house-centric customer model on Microsoft Dynamics 365 CE: auto-routing by skill + proficiency, online booking, ~30 aggregator feeds via BirdDog, three API-funded customer-incentive engines.
  • The scope is end-to-end now — most of the value sits in Grant's lane. Original Charter scope was call-center + lead-intake. Expanded scope reaches all the way through sale, install, and service.
  • "Most technical project setbacks are due to non-technical reasons." (Jeff Loven, Cirrius) The delivery posture is change management and decision velocity, not Salesforce expertise alone.
